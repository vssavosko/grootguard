FUSION

The prevention terminal for wildfires. Every layer is public — fuel, weather, infrastructure, people. Nobody crossed them. We rank every cell of Spain by what its loss would cascade into, and turn a prevention budget into an optimal allocation.

01

The pitch in three lines

Hook (rewritten 20 Sep — open on LIVE, the red dots first): "These red dots are not a simulation. This is Spain burning right now — this minute, from the same Copernicus satellite feed the fire services watch. Around every dot our terminal already knows what's downwind: the fuel from ESA satellites, the people from census grids, the hospital behind the substation. And it knows what Spain can answer with: ten flyable water-bombers — ten, for seventeen regions, and the replacements land in 2028. The three largest fires in the 56-year record book all burned in the last fourteen months. FUSION is the screen that decides what we protect first — before the next dot appears."

Use case (rewritten 20 Sep — a day at the desk, both clocks): the civil-protection duty officer opens FUSION at 08:00. LIVE is the workbench: real satellite hotspots on a map of 14,002 real cells, the last 10 observed days and the 10-day forecast on one hourly bar — and the terminal has already turned tomorrow's risk ranking into a staging plan: which sectors get the ten flyable amphibians, the ten BRIF heli-crews, the five UME battalions, at which alert level. Every ring is a recommendation with a number on it. Then the same screen takes the money: the prevention slider places grazing, burns, clearing and hardening where they buy the most future — and PREDICTION shows what that same euro does across three futures: 46% of exposed people protected if the programme is sustained, 6% if it's abandoned. One surface, both clocks — the fire this week and the fuel this decade — every choice signed, dated, auditable.

Climax: Drag the budget slider. €400M pours into goats, burns, machines and hardening — and the screen promises a band, not a number: 13–23% of national risk removed, with the honest corner where prevention does not pay on display. That corner is why a minister can trust the rest of the screen.

Judge counters (memorize)

  • "Doesn't EFFIS / the state already have this?" (THE grill question — rewritten 20 Sep after adversarial research) — "Yes: EGIF since 1968, AEMET's new 1-km IPIF index, mandated risk cartography — the state has excellent hazard data, and we say so. What it does NOT have — and the Pacto de Estado itself diagnoses this, proposing a 'National Plan for Interconnection and Interoperability' — is the fusion across silos and the auditable link from risk map → euro → executed measure → verified outcome. Galicia's own 2018 recommendations went unexecuted; 80% of high-risk municipalities lack their mandated plans (Greenpeace). The gap is execution and accountability, not information — and that is exactly the layer we sell."
  • "Why a substation over a school?" — We don't price buildings, we trace failure: a substation's score is the hospitals and households that go dark when it burns. Criticality cascade, not price tags.
  • "Prevention isn't real-time." — The queue re-ranks with this week's forecast. A dry-wind week moves a zone four ranks up. Real-time data → real-time decisions, exactly per brief.
  • "Isn't fire natural here?" — A fire is a reset or a wipe. 2025's ledger: over 65% of NW Iberia burned at high severity — regime-breaking, not regenerative — and 44% of northern Spain's burns hit Natura 2000 habitats. Roughly one third reset, two thirds wipe. Our map shrugs at fire-adapted scrub and spends money only on the wipes. And the regime flip is dated and citable: early 1970s — fire frequency ×2, burned area ×10, fuel-limited → drought-driven (Pausas & Fernández-Muñoz 2012).

04

Scoring model

Every constant below is a declared assumption — they live in one assumptions.json, are shown on the methodology panel, and the cost figure is a UI slider. No number is presented as measured truth.

# per H3 cell i
F_i = 1.0*tree_frac + 0.8*shrub_frac + 0.5*grass_frac          # fuel, 0..1
W_i = clamp(0.6 + 0.25*z(t_max) + 0.25*z(-RH_min)
          + 0.25*z(wind_max) + 0.25*z(days_since_rain), 0.6, 1.6)  # condition
# WUI exposure filter: city cores don't burn in wildfires; bare/desertified land can't burn
expF_i = clamp((F_i − 0.05)×1.6, 0.03, 1)     # exposed share of people & value per cell
pop_exposed_i = pop_i × expF_i                # a Gran Vía flat is safe by definition;
                                              # a village in the pines is ~fully exposed
C_i = pop_exposed_i/8k + 0.5*Σ(pop_exposed_neighbors)/8k        # exposure
    + Σ assets: hospital 10 · power_plant 9 · substation 8
                + 0.002*pop_within_10km        # ← the cascade term
                school 4 · industrial 3 · rail/motorway 2
    + 6*natura2000_frac + 3*broadleaf_frac      # ← ecological capital
      # fire-adapted scrub with no assets scores ~0 → the map itself
      # answers "isn't fire natural?": a reset is cheap, a wipe is not
M_i = fuel-moisture proxy 0..1 (ERA5 soil moisture / days-since-rain)   # the slow layer
COMBUSTION_i = F_i × W_i × (0.4 + 0.6×(1−M_i))   # biomass × weather × dryness
PRIORITY_i = COMBUSTION_i × C_i        # god's-eye fusion; only where F_i > 0.15
# God's-Eye layer deck (map views): ECONOMICS=C · BIOMASS=F · WEATHER=W
#   · MOISTURE=M · COMBUSTION=H · FUSION=PRIORITY (only FUSION shows allocation)
# budget optimizer (the demo climax) — PORTFOLIO allocator, see table below
STRAT = 0.25                          # treat the strategic fuel-break network, never every ha
cost_i,instr = burnable_ha_i × STRAT × €/ha(instrument)
allocate greedily by PRIORITY_i × eff / cost, respecting instrument CAPS
outputs: risk removed as worst–best BAND · exposed people covered ·
         workforce (shepherds / burn specialists / forest workers)

The cascade term is the whole thesis in one line: a substation is worth the population it serves, not the steel it's made of. Say that sentence to the judges.

The honest ratio — flow vs flow, never billions-vs-millions (grilled 19 Sep, late)

Three numbers, three time bases — the product shows all three, labeled, and never blurs them:

  • Exposure secured (stock, €B) — assets standing in treated zones. Insurance language. NOT annual savings.
  • Expected annual loss avoided (flow, €M/yr) — the only number comparable to the budget: real burn rate (EGIF 1968–2023 avg ≈ 146,000 ha/yr SCAN) × declared €3k–15k/ha damage proxy = €0.4–2.2B/yr expected damage × the share of risk the allocation removes. Spend annualized by cycle (graze 1y · burn 3y · clear 4y · WUI 10y).
  • CORRECTED 20 Sep, demo morning measured from the running model — the ratio is ≈ 0.4–3.1:1, NOT 2–8:1. The earlier 2–8:1 was a spec-time estimate that the built model does not reproduce; the sim was additionally flooring its own worst case to "1:1" via Math.max(1,…), i.e. displaying a better number than it computed. Both are fixed. Measured at the default €400M: annualized spend €164M, avoided €58–504M/yr ⇒ 0.4–3.1:1 (€100M budget: 0.4–3.6:1). The band is corner-to-corner: the low end pairs worst-case effectiveness with the cheapest damage assumption (€3k/ha of burnt scrub) — at that corner prevention does not repay inside one year; the high end pairs best-case effectiveness with €15k/ha. Both corners understate: post-fire erosion and flood losses are not counted at all.
  • The 1:100 quote stays a quote (marginal anecdote), never our claim. Pitch line: "Between 0.4 and 3 times per year depending on which corner of the assumptions you stand in — and we show you the corner where it doesn't pay, because a number that only ever flatters us is worth nothing to you. Every assumption is a slider." Why this is a stronger pitch: every other team will quote the €1→€100 folklore; we are the only ones whose screen argues against itself. That is the whole RISK TAKEN thesis in one number.
  • Finance discipline (19 Sep, late night): all euros in constant-2026 real terms, labor-heavy costs escalated with real wages (Baumol), never flat nominal over decades. Three money numbers kept apart: year-1 cash (appropriation now) · steady-state €/yr (cycles settled: graze 1y / burn 3y / clear 4y) · CAPEX (WUI hardening = 10-yr infrastructure asset — amortized/bond-able, not an annual expense). Pathway budget paths, anchored: WORST replays the real 2009–24 decay (−2%/yr real; €364M→€144M) · AVG flat-real · BEST sustained wage-indexed. NPV over the 60-yr horizon uses the EU 3% real SDR with a declining-rate sensitivity (the SDR choice alone swings 2086 values ×7.7).

Final UX — three views (locked 19 Sep, evening; time bases finalized 19 Sep night)

Top-level navigation: HISTORIC · LIVE · PREDICTION. One decision surface (LIVE), one proof surface (HISTORIC), one consequence surface (PREDICTION). Three time bases: HISTORIC = 1900 → now (weekly) · LIVE = D-10 … D+10 dynamic range (last 10 days observed + next 10 days forecast — the honest window of weather knowledge; hourly diurnal cycle inside it) · PREDICTION = 2026 → 2086 horizon slider, single-horizon view: only the latest prediction for the selected year is ever shown, past runs never stack. Evidence continuity: documented fire scars carry across all three views under one rule (85% faded at 15 years; risk prior = fade × ~5-yr regrowth) — in PREDICTION they keep fading as the horizon advances until the map honestly declares "evidence gone, model alone"; no future fires are ever invented. Prediction pathways differ in ONE lever: whether the prevention program is sustained (fuel path), against a declared warming trend.

Century replay, 1900 → mid-2026, weekly: early decades run dim (fuel-limited grazed landscape — declared model per fusion-research anchors), the map fills with fuel after 1950 (exodus + 3M ha plantations), the regime flips ~1972 (Pausas 2012), and the record era ends on Burgohondo 2026 — the largest fire in the Spanish record. Arm 2 — prevention (grazing / rx burn / clearing / WUI), each chip tagged with its horizon (short–mid / mid / mid–long / long): started today, it pays on the PREDICTION horizon — it cannot stop this week's fire, and the UI says so.
